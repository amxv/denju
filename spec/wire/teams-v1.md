# Denju teams and transfers wire contract v1

Teams are shared namespace authorities built on the same stable `namespaces` and `resources` identity model as users. A namespace slug is globally unique across users and teams, so `@acme` can identify exactly one authority and team-owned resources keep ordinary locators such as `@acme/review` and `@acme/packs/core`.

## Team roles and namespace authority

Every team has exactly one `owner` membership and zero or more `maintainer` or `member` memberships. The stable wire role strings are:

- `owner`;
- `maintainer`;
- `member`.

Owners and maintainers may publish into the team namespace. A member may publish only when the team's `members_can_publish` setting is enabled. That switch is team-wide; v1 does not add per-skill team ACLs.

Only the owner may change member roles, remove members, or change `members_can_publish`. Maintainers may invite new members but may not invite maintainers. The owner may invite either maintainers or members. The owner membership cannot be removed or changed through the role endpoint; ownership succession is a separate lifecycle operation outside this phase.

## Team endpoints

- `GET /v1/teams` lists teams for the authenticated user with that user's role and the current publish setting.
- `POST /v1/teams` creates a globally unique team namespace and the caller's single owner membership atomically.
- `GET /v1/teams/show?team=...` returns team membership and settings to a current team member.
- `POST /v1/teams/invites` creates a short-lived invitation credential for an allowed role.
- `POST /v1/teams/invites/revoke` revokes an unused invitation.
- `POST /v1/teams/join` consumes one invitation credential and creates exactly one membership.
- `POST /v1/teams/members/role` changes a non-owner membership role.
- `POST /v1/teams/members/remove` removes a non-owner membership.
- `POST /v1/teams/settings` changes `members_can_publish`.
- `POST /v1/resources/transfer` moves one personally owned skill or pack into a team while preserving stable resource identity.

Every team mutation and transfer uses UUIDv7 operation identity plus an endpoint-domain canonical request hash. A committed exact retry replays its stored response. Reusing an operation ID with different intent is rejected.

## Invitation credentials

Invitation codes are bearer secrets generated by the client. The registry receives and persists only `SHA-256(code)`, never the raw code. Codes expire 24 hours after creation, are single-use, and may be revoked before use.

Join locks the invitation row before checking expiry, revocation, or prior use and before inserting membership. Membership creation and marking the invitation used commit in one PostgreSQL transaction, so concurrent consumers cannot both join with the same code.

## Team skill private workspaces

Team ownership never creates a shared live draft. Every authorized publisher edits a separate private workspace identified by `(resource_id, workspace_user_id)` and has a workspace-local generation for compare-and-swap.

A publisher workspace may be provisioned only from the latest immutable team release. Another maintainer's unpublished workspace is never a seed. Consequently, the first unpublished import is visible only to its importer until a team release exists.

Ordinary private edits advance only that publisher's workspace generation and immutable revision history. They do not advance the shared resource generation, emit shared desired-state wakes, or expose draft bytes to another team member.

Publishing attempts to release the caller's private head against the current team release. If another publisher released first:

- when the latest release is already an ancestor of the caller's head, publication may proceed;
- otherwise the registry records the two immutable heads plus merge base as a conflict scoped to the caller's workspace;
- the client performs the existing deterministic three-way merge, commits a clean merge as a new private revision, and retries publication;
- a real content conflict preserves both heads and pauses only that publisher for explicit resolution.

The latest immutable release is the shared team read surface. A team publish is team-private by default. `--public` makes the resource globally public, and public visibility remains sticky until explicit unpublish returns it to team-private visibility.

Removing a member's publish permission removes only that user's team private workspaces and unresolved workspace conflicts. Immutable revisions and team releases remain intact.

## Team-private read and sharing boundary

Current team members may read immutable releases of team-private skills. Only the publisher who owns a private workspace may read revisions reachable solely from that workspace.

Creating new per-skill private shares on a team skill is rejected because team membership is the team ACL. A private share created while a skill was personally owned survives a later team transfer by stable resource ID, but becomes release-only: the recipient may follow immutable team releases and can never read future maintainer drafts.

## Team packs

A team-private pack is readable by current team members. Every pack member must be readable by the pack's full audience, not merely by the maintainer authoring the mutation.

For a team-private pack, a private member is therefore valid only when it is a released skill owned by the same team; public skill releases are also valid. A personally private skill that only the editing maintainer can read is rejected atomically. Public pack rules remain stricter: every member must be globally public.

Transferring a personal pack into a team re-resolves all authored pack member intent against the destination team's full audience before moving ownership. If any member would become unreadable, the transfer fails without changing owner, locator, redirects, or transfer-operation state.

## Stable personal-to-team transfer

`POST /v1/resources/transfer` accepts only an active resource currently owned by the caller's personal namespace and a destination namespace in which the caller has team publish authority. The transfer keeps the existing resource UUID and all relationships keyed by it, including immutable history, releases, subscriptions, shares, fork provenance, proposals, pack references, and other resource-ID relationships.

The transaction:

1. locks and generation-checks the source resource;
2. rejects a same-kind destination slug collision;
3. revalidates team-pack audience when transferring a pack;
4. checks destination logical blob quota before moving reachability;
5. moves namespace blob reachability while preserving resource blob reachability;
6. creates an old-locator redirect to the same stable resource;
7. changes only the resource owner namespace and advances resource generation;
8. records the idempotent transfer outcome and shared-resource wake.

Collision, audience, quota, or reachability failures roll the entire transaction back. There is never a partially moved resource, dangling redirect, or committed transfer outcome for a failed attempt.

For a transferred skill, the transferor's current personal workspace becomes that same user's private team workspace without copying or publishing it. Other authorized publishers can provision their own workspace only from a team release.

## Deferred team lifecycle

Team ownership succession, team deletion, assigned/enforced packs, and the owner-renewal/delete lifecycle are intentionally outside this contract. Team-owned skill and pack deletion remains blocked until those owner-only lifecycle rules land.
